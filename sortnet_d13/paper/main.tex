\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present a comparator network on 27 channels with 152 comparators and 13 layers. It improves the size of the best known 13-layer 27-input network on Dobbelaere's list of sorting networks from 153 to 152 at equal depth. The network is the published 153-comparator network with a single comparator, $(23,26)$ in layer~7, deleted. That comparator is not redundant in the classical sense---it changes some binary inputs---so standard redundancy stripping does not find it; an exact removability test does. Correctness was established by two independently written exhaustive verifiers over all $2^{27}=134{,}217{,}728$ binary inputs and confirmed by a third algorithm (output-set propagation). An exhaustive single-comparator deletion test over all 54 networks of the list finds no other removable comparator, and no pair of comparators can be removed from the new network or from the 28-input 159-comparator 13-layer network.

The network was found while attacking the problem that motivated the work: a 13-layer sorting network on 29, 30, 31 or 32 channels, for which the best known depth is 14. That attack failed, and we document how. Wang's 2025 construction of a 13-layer 28-input network (stacked reflection-symmetric block prefixes, a greedy sixth layer, SAT for layers 7 to 13) was reproduced end to end and transferred faithfully to 30 channels (16+14 blocks) and 32 channels (16+16 blocks and the 5-cube). For every one of the 28 six-layer prefixes tested---22 on 30 channels from three families and 6 on 32 channels from two families---the SAT instance for a reflection-symmetric completion in seven further layers is unsatisfiable, each proof taking between 500 and 1800 seconds with MiniSat; one instance was re-proved without the optional normal-form constraints. We also show that Wang's 159 comparators are optimal for his first prefix among reflection-symmetric completions, and that a greedy seventh layer destroys completability even for Wang's own completable prefixes, so fast negatives of that kind carry no information. Depth 13 for 29 to 32 inputs remains open; we give a ranked list of what a follow-up should try. All code, networks, run logs and verification transcripts are in the accompanying repository.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A comparator network on $n$ channels is a fixed sequence of compare--exchange operations; it is a sorting network if it sorts every input. Two cost measures are classical: the size (number of comparators) and the depth (number of parallel layers, each layer a set of comparators on pairwise disjoint channels). Optimal values are known only for small $n$: optimal depth up to $n=17$ \cite{BZ14,CCEMS19} and optimal size up to $n=12$ \cite{Harder20}. Beyond that, the state of the art is a table of best known upper bounds maintained by Dobbelaere \cite{Dobb}, whose entries come from classical constructions \cite{Batcher68,Knuth}, from heuristic search (SorterHunter \cite{SorterHunter}) and from SAT-based prefix completion \cite{Ehlers17,Wang25}.

At the time of this work (list dated 7 November 2025) the depth entries for 25 to 32 inputs read: 25, 26, 27 and 28 inputs at depth 13, and 29, 30, 31 and 32 inputs at depth 14, with lower bounds of 10 for all of them. The depth-13 entries for 27 and 28 are credited to Wang \cite{Wang25}, who found a reflection-symmetric 28-input network with 159 comparators in 13 layers; the depth-14 entries for 29 to 32 come from Batcher's odd--even merge \cite{Batcher68}. The question we set out to answer was whether Wang's method extends to 29 to 32 inputs, which would lower four table entries by one layer at once: a 32-input network of depth 13 yields 31-, 30- and 29-input networks of the same depth by channel deletion.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A 27-input sorting network with 152 comparators and 13 layers (Section~\ref{sec:n27}), verified by two independent exhaustive checks, improving the list entry $(153,13)$.
\item An exhaustive single-comparator deletion analysis of all 54 networks on the list (Section~\ref{sec:sweep}): exactly one comparator anywhere on the list is deletable, namely the one above. Pair deletions from the 27/152 and 28/159 networks are impossible.
\item A size-optimality statement for Wang's prefix (Section~\ref{sec:sizesat}): no reflection-symmetric 13-layer completion of his first six-layer prefix has fewer than 76 suffix comparators, so 159 is optimal for that prefix under symmetry.
\item Exact negative results for the transfer of Wang's construction to 30 and 32 channels (Sections~\ref{sec:n30} and~\ref{sec:n32}): 28 six-layer prefixes from five families, all with unsatisfiable seven-layer completion instances, plus a control run showing that the verdict does not depend on the normal-form constraints.
\item A methodological observation (Section~\ref{sec:greedy7}): adding a greedy seventh layer to Wang's own eight completable 28-channel prefixes makes all eight instances unsatisfiable in under ten seconds. Greedy minimum-output extension beyond layer~6 is therefore the wrong tool, and negatives obtained that way say nothing about the underlying six-layer prefixes.
\item A complete, dependency-light reimplementation of the pipeline (prefix generation with symmetric subsumption, stacking, greedy extension, the SAT encoding of \cite{CCEMS19} with additional controls, decoding with untangling, stripping, re-layering), two independent verifiers with a test suite, and every run's parameters and log, so that each claim can be rerun.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Rules of evidence.}
Everything labelled \VER{} passed both verifiers on all $2^n$ binary inputs. Everything labelled \NEG{} is an exact statement about specific prefixes under a stated completion model, backed by a solver's proof of unsatisfiability. Everything that timed out, was killed or was not run is labelled \INC{}. A timeout is never treated as evidence of nonexistence, and no running time or table value in this paper was adjusted from what the logs record.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Networks.} Channels are numbered $0$ to $n-1$. A comparator $(i,j)$ with $i<j$ replaces the values on channels $i$ and $j$ by their minimum on $i$ and their maximum on $j$. A layer is a set of comparators on pairwise disjoint channels; a network of depth $d$ is a sequence of $d$ layers. Its size is the total number of comparators. Following \dob, a network is written one layer per line as a list of pairs.

\paragraph{Zero--one principle.} A network sorts all inputs from a totally ordered set if and only if it sorts all $2^n$ binary inputs \cite[\S5.3.4]{Knuth}. All verification in this paper is over binary inputs.

\paragraph{Output sets.} For a prefix $P$ (the first $k$ layers) let $\out(P)$ be the set of binary vectors $P$ produces from all $2^n$ binary inputs. A suffix $S$ completes $P$ to a sorting network if and only if $S$ sorts every vector in $\out(P)$. The cardinality $|\out(P)|$ is the standard measure of a prefix's quality: smaller sets give smaller SAT instances and, empirically, are easier to complete. The output set of a first layer with $m$ comparators has $3^m 2^{n-2m}$ elements. Two reference values are Dedekind numbers: a 4-cube on 16 channels (four layers of dimension-wise comparators) has 168 outputs and a 5-cube on 32 channels has 7581.

\paragraph{Reflection symmetry.} The reflection $\rho(i)=n-1-i$ maps a comparator $(i,j)$ to $(\rho(j),\rho(i))$. A network is reflection-symmetric if every layer is mapped to itself. Symmetry halves the number of free variables in a SAT encoding; it is the restriction Wang used, and the one under which every negative result in this paper is stated. A network on an odd number of channels cannot be reflection-symmetric layer by layer, since a comparator on the middle channel has no disjoint mirror image; this is why a direct search on 29 channels needs a relaxed symmetry and why 29- and 31-input networks are usually obtained from 30- and 32-input ones by channel deletion.

\paragraph{Channel deletion.} Fixing the input of the last channel to $+\infty$ (or of the first to $-\infty$) makes every comparator on that channel a pass-through, so removing those comparators and renumbering gives a sorting network on $n-1$ channels of the same depth and smaller size \cite[\S5.3.4]{Knuth}; an interior channel can be deleted in the same way after relabelling channels along the path the fixed value takes.

\paragraph{Redundant and removable comparators.} A comparator is \emph{redundant} if it never swaps its inputs on any output of the preceding prefix; redundant comparators are removed by standard stripping. A comparator can, however, swap inputs on some prefix outputs and still be unnecessary for sorting, because later comparators repair the difference. We call such a comparator \emph{removable}. Removability must be decided by an exact test (does the network without it still sort?), which is what Section~\ref{sec:n27} does. Completability of a prefix is not monotone under adding comparators \cite[\S5.3.4, exercise~21]{Knuth}: adding comparators can destroy the possibility of completing a prefix within a given depth, which is the mechanism behind Section~\ref{sec:greedy7}.

\paragraph{Re-layering.} A network's depth is the number of layers after greedy re-layering (each comparator placed in the earliest layer after the last comparator sharing one of its channels). All depths reported here were obtained this way, and all sizes were reported after stripping.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Two independent verifiers}\label{sec:verifiers}

\emph{Verifier~A} (\path{src/verify_c.c}, C with OpenMP) is a bit-sliced simulator. Sixty-four binary inputs are packed into one 64-bit word per channel (the low six bits of the input index select the lane; the remaining $n-6$ bits are constant across a word), every comparator becomes one \textsc{and} and one \textsc{or}, and an output word is sorted if and only if for every adjacent pair of channels $w_c \wedge \neg w_{c+1}$ is zero. All $2^{n-6}$ words are processed, and the number of failing inputs and the first failing input are reported. It exits with status~0 if the network sorts, 1 if not, 2 on a parse error.

\emph{Verifier~B} (\path{src/verify_py.py}, Python~3 and numpy) has its own parser and two algorithms unrelated to Verifier~A's. Mode \texttt{exhaustive} simulates all $2^n$ inputs as integers in chunks of $2^{22}$ and tests each output for the form $0^a1^b$. Mode \texttt{outputset} computes the set of reachable binary vectors layer by layer (product construction after the first layer, then comparator application and deduplication) and checks that the final set consists exactly of the $n+1$ sorted vectors; it also reports the size of the reachable set after every layer. The option \texttt{--mode all} runs both.

\emph{Tests} (\path{tests/test_verifiers.py}, four tests). Both verifiers accept every network of \dob{} with $n\le 20$ and reject each of those networks with its last comparator deleted; both accept Wang's 28-input network and reject it with one comparator removed; both reject malformed inputs with a parse error, accept the one-comparator 2-sorter and a three-layer 4-sorter, and reject the same 4-channel network without its last layer. A second suite (\path{tests/test_pipeline.py}, two tests) exercises the search pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}: it reproduces Wang's counts of non-redundant reflection-symmetric 12-channel prefixes (41 at depth~2, 1502 at depth~3), finds three depth-7 networks on 10 channels from two-layer prefixes by SAT that pass both verifiers, and proves depth~6 unsatisfiable for the same prefixes (depth~7 is optimal for 10 inputs \cite{Parberry91}).

\subsection{The search pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

The pipeline in \path{src/snt/} (C++20, no external dependencies) reimplements Wang's method with additional controls. Its stages are sub-commands of one binary.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,font=\ttfamily]
\item[gen] Generate-and-prune of reflection-symmetric prefixes layer by layer, with subsumption under channel permutations and under reflect-and-complement (the lemma of Bundala and Z\'avodn\'y \cite{BZ14}), keeping the $k$ best prefixes by output-set size per layer when a \texttt{--keep} limit is given.
\item[stack] Place a symmetric prefix on an outer block of channels and another on the inner block (\emph{nested}), or a block and its reflection side by side (\emph{mirrored}); the output set of the stack is the product of the block output sets.
\item[extend] Greedy addition of one comparator at a time (a comparator together with its mirror image) to a new layer, keeping the 64 best partial layers by output-set size---Wang's greedy sixth layer.
\item[cnf] The SAT encoding of Codish, Cruz-Filipe, Ehlers, M\"uller and Schneider-Kamp \cite{CCEMS19} as ported by Wang: comparator variables $g_{k,i,j}$ with mirror identification, used-channel variables, ``one up / one down'' propagation variables per input and channel, a per-input window encoding after a channel permutation that minimises the sum of windows (leading zeros and trailing ones of each prefix output become constants), the necessary last-layer constraints (only adjacent comparators in the last layer; span at most~3 in the second-to-last layer, with the implied structure), and the optional normal-form constraints $\psi_1$ and $\psi_3$ (no two adjacent idle channels in the last layer, and the corresponding forms for the second-to-last layer). Flags added for this work: \texttt{--no\_nf} drops the normal-form constraints (the ``necessary constraints only'' model); \texttt{--open\_last} leaves the last prefix layer partially specified for the solver to complete; \texttt{--max\_comps~$K$} adds a cardinality constraint on the number of suffix comparators (Sinz's sequential counter \cite{Sinz05}; a mirrored pair counts two); \texttt{--subnet~$w$} produces the Bundala--Z\'avodn\'y subnetwork relaxation that keeps only prefix outputs of window at most $w$.
\item[decode] Read a model, undo the channel permutation by Knuth's untangling (when a comparator would be reversed, swap the labels of its two channels for all later comparators; valid for a suffix because the prefix output set contains all sorted vectors), strip redundant comparators and re-layer.
\item[prune] For each comparator (or each pair with \texttt{--pairs}), delete it and decide exactly by the output-set method whether the remaining network still sorts, caching the reachable set after each layer.
\end{description}

A driver script (\path{src/run_search.sh}) runs a whole experiment: CNF generation for the $k$ best prefixes of a library, parallel solving with a time budget, decoding, and both verifiers on every decoded network. It writes \path{runs/<name>/log.txt} with instance sizes and per-instance outcomes and times. Every run reported below has such a log; the CNF and solution files themselves are not kept in the repository.

As a sanity check of the cardinality option: from the best two-layer prefix on 10 channels (9 comparators), a suffix of at most 22 comparators is satisfiable (size 31, matching the list entry $(31,7)$) and a suffix of at most 20 is unsatisfiable.

Our own encoder was validated on Wang's prefixes. \subsection{A greedy seventh layer destroys completability}\label{sec:greedy7}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:greedy7}
Wang's eight six-layer prefixes are all completable in seven further layers (Section~\ref{sec:repro}). Extending each by one greedy layer chosen exactly as his sixth layer (comparator pairs added while the output set shrinks, keep 64) reduces the output sets from 928 to between 530 and 541. Asked for a reflection-symmetric completion of these seven-layer prefixes in six layers, MiniSat proves all eight instances unsatisfiable, in 4, 5, 5, 5, 5, 4, 8 and 9\,s (\path{runs/calib_wang_L7_minisat}).
\end{observation}

Small output sets are therefore not a proxy for completability past layer~6, and every fast negative in this paper that involved a greedy seventh layer (runs B, D, E, K30 and K32 below) is uninformative about the prefixes preceding that layer. The fair test of a six-layer prefix is Wang's configuration itself---six fixed layers plus seven SAT layers---and that is the configuration used for every \NEG{} label on a six-layer prefix.

\section{A 27-input network with 152 comparators and 13 layers}\label{sec:n27}

\subsection{Derivation}

An exact single-comparator deletion test (\texttt{prune}, Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) was run on the published depth-13 networks for 22 to 28 inputs. Exactly one comparator on these seven networks is removable: comparator $(23,26)$ in layer~7 of the network listed as ``27 inputs, 153 CEs, 13 layers''. Removing it leaves a 27-channel network with 152 comparators and 13 non-empty layers. The comparator is not redundant in the classical sense: it swaps its inputs on some outputs of the first six layers, so the network after layer~7 has a different output set with and without it, and only the exact test discovers that the last six layers sort both. After the deletion no further single comparator, and no pair of comparators (all 11\,476 pairs tested), can be removed.

\subsection{No other network on the list has a removable comparator (\NEG)}\label{sec:sweep}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sweep}
Of the 54 networks on \dob{} (version of 7 November 2025), exactly one has a removable comparator: the 27/153/13 network, from which exactly the comparator of Theorem~\ref{thm:n27} can be removed. After that removal nothing further is removable. No pair of comparators can be removed from the resulting 27/152/13 network (11\,476 pairs) or from Wang's 28/159/13 network (12\,561 pairs).
\end{proposition}

The single-deletion sweep is \path{runs/prune_all.log} (the 32-input network takes about four minutes); the pair tests are \path{runs/pairs_n27_152.log} and \path{runs/pairs_n28_159.log}. \subsection{Statement}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:n30}
For each of the 22 six-layer prefixes of runs A2, F, F5, F$'$ and F$''$ (2 from family S, 12 from N4, 8 from NL), there is no reflection-symmetric comparator network of 7 layers that sorts every output of the prefix. Hence none of these prefixes extends to a reflection-symmetric 13-layer sorting network on 30 channels.
\end{theorem}

Each proof of unsatisfiability took between 500 and 1800\,s.

\subsection{Soundness of the negatives}\label{sec:sound}

Each \NEG{} label rests on two points.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The CNF is a correct model of ``a symmetric suffix of the given depth sorts every prefix output''. This was validated positively: the same encoder produces satisfiable instances and verified networks on Wang's 28-channel prefixes (Section~\ref{sec:encoder}) and finds the depth optimum on 10 channels (Section~\ref{sec:verifiers}).
\item The restrictions on the last two layers (last layer adjacent comparators only; second-to-last layer span at most~3 with its implications; Lemmas~4 and~6 of \cite{CCEMS19}) are necessary for the non-redundant form of any sorting network of the given depth, and removing redundant comparators preserves reflection symmetry, because a comparator and its mirror image are redundant together. They therefore exclude no symmetric completion. The normal-form constraints $\psi_1$ and $\psi_3$ are proven in \cite{CCEMS19} for unrestricted networks; rather than argue their soundness under the symmetry restriction, the control run re-proved run F's first instance with them removed (654\,s instead of 520\,s). The \NEG{} labels for the other 21 prefixes still include the normal-form constraints; a reviewer who wants to remove that dependence can rerun any of them with \texttt{--no\_nf} at about the same cost.
\end{enumerate}

What the negatives do not say: nothing about non-symmetric completions (run H timed out), nothing about other sixth layers on the same five-layer stacks (run G was killed; the structured sixth layers were not solved), and nothing about prefixes outside these families.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why the transfer fails where Wang's succeeded.} Two quantitative differences stand out. First, the starting point: after five layers Wang's stacks have 2905 outputs, ours 5478 to 7581, because the 14-channel and second 16-channel blocks are far from the 35-output quality of his exhaustively optimised 12-channel block. The best five-layer 14-channel symmetric prefix we could produce has 66 outputs; a 14-channel block would need fewer than 81 outputs merely to beat the seed family's 6723 and far fewer to match Wang's ratio. Second, the hand-over to SAT: after the greedy sixth layer we hand the solver 1693 to 1853 outputs, where every successful completion in the literature handed it at most about 1200. Whether this is a barrier or an artefact of the greedy sixth layer is the central open question. Ehlers' 24-channel result is instructive: according to our reading of his account (recorded in \path{NOTES.md}), his completable prefix had a SAT-chosen rank-aligned sixth layer (1154 to 479 outputs) that a comparator-at-a-time greedy does not find, and he observes that greedy prefix construction typically fails because of a few bad decisions, suggesting limited-discrepancy search around the greedy choice.

\paragraph{Block completability does not discriminate.} We tested whether each five-layer block prefix completes, under symmetry, to a depth-optimal sorter of the block alone (16 channels in depth~9, 14 in depth~9, 12 in depth~8). The two 83-output VV16 variants split (one is symmetric-completable, the other only non-symmetrically), the 14-channel prefixes split, and among Wang's eight successful prefixes the inner 16-blocks split four to four. A depth-13 network on 32 channels cannot first sort two 16-blocks (9 layers each, in parallel) and then merge them (at least 5 layers, since the middle output depends on all 32 inputs): sorting and merging must interleave, and in Wang's network the cross-block comparators begin in layer~6.

\paragraph{What a follow-up should try}, ranked by expected value per CPU hour:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Larger budgets for the fair instances. Per our reading of the literature, Codish et al.\ spent up to about 97\,000\,s on single instances and Bundala and Z\'avodn\'y up to 24 hours; our budgets of 1800 to 7200\,s are small for instances two to four times larger. Run G (five-layer stack, eight free layers, 602\,000 variables and 21.8 million clauses) and run H (non-symmetric suffix) are the natural candidates; on four cores that is days.
\item A SAT-scored beam or limited-discrepancy search for layer~6 instead of greedy minimum output set: keep the 200 to 500 best sixth-layer candidates, sweep them with a 900\,s budget each, and invest hours only in survivors.
\item Better block prefixes: a true optimum for 14 channels (our libraries were keep-limited), blocks from the 61-comparator 16-input network (110 outputs but a different structure), a beam-searched sixth layer on the 5-cube for 32 channels, and 15+15 mirrored stacks once a fast non-symmetric 15-channel generator is available.
\item Non-symmetric suffixes throughout (the instance doubles; run H is the template).
\item Encoding: add the co-saturation constraints of \cite{CCEMS19} (their Lemma~8) and the solver settings used there (probing preprocessing, clause decay 0.9999).
\item Cube-and-conquer on the layer-7 variables of the best instance, to use many cores.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item All negatives are for reflection-symmetric suffixes and specific prefixes; they do not bound depth 13 for 30 or 32 inputs in general.
\item The normal-form constraints were removed in one control instance only; the other 21 negatives on 30 channels and all negatives on 32 channels include them.
\item Wall-clock times were measured on a shared four-core machine and are not comparable to each other or to the literature at better than a factor of two; the run A timeouts show the effect.
\item The 14-channel prefix libraries were keep-limited, not exhaustive; a better 14-channel block may exist.
\item The list entries used as baselines are those of 7 November 2025; a later update of the list would change the improvement claim, not the network.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and confidence}\label{sec:conclusion}

The claim that the 152-comparator network sorts rests on two independent exhaustive checks of all $2^{27}$ binary inputs by programs written separately, one of which also confirms the result by output-set propagation, and both of which were validated against every network of \dob{} with $n\le 20$ and against deliberately broken networks. The depth is the number of non-empty layers in the listing (13) and the size the number of comparators in it (152); neither can be reduced by stripping, single deletion or pair deletion. The improvement claim rests on the list entry $(153,13)$ for 27 inputs as of 7 November 2025.

The reproductions of Wang's bound are equally solid and add nothing new. The \NEG{} results are exact statements under a stated model whose two premises---a correct encoding and sound last-layer restrictions---were each tested positively; the one residual dependence, on the normal-form constraints under symmetry, was removed for one instance and can be removed for the others at known cost. Whether a 13-layer sorting network exists on 29, 30, 31 or 32 channels remains open. The evidence here says only that the most direct transfer of the one method known to reach depth 13 beyond 26 channels does not work with the block prefixes and greedy sixth layers we could produce, and that the next attempt should change the sixth layer, the blocks, or the budget rather than repeat these runs.

A skeptical reader should rerun the two verifier commands of Section~\ref{sec:repro-cmds} first, then the control instance of run F.
